# Modern French Interior

## Preservation default

For an image edit, preserve camera, walls, openings, ceiling levels, fixed built-ins, and circulation unless the user explicitly grants structural freedom. If the source shell is important or prior edits drifted, combine this skill with lock-architecture-shell.

## Design principles

- Preserve modern spatial clarity; add French character through proportion, furniture lineage, material craft, and collected objects.
- Use one clean stepped cornice and sparse complete wall panels only where they terminate cleanly.
- Keep mouldings the same color as the wall and readable through shadow.
- Never run mouldings into cabinets, windows, art, or stone surfaces. Maintain intentional margins and closed rectangles.
- Prefer natural oak herringbone, dark walnut, honed ivory limestone, warm-white plaster, oatmeal linen, cognac leather, matte ceramic, opaline glass, and restrained stainless steel.
- Select elegant French-modern seating: tailored bowed backs, shaped arms, visible timber bases, and controlled curves. Avoid bulky blob sofas and generic boucle.
- Use rectangular stone coffee tables, cane or timber lounge chairs, and sparse Parisian decor.
- Keep the ceiling flat unless the existing architecture differs. Use a few small pin lights and one sculptural fixture; avoid LED coves.
- Choose herringbone board scale and laying direction from room proportions and thresholds; preserve existing floor levels and transitions.

## Styling

Use art books, plaster or bisque sculpture, linen cushions, a cashmere throw, clear glass, white flowers, and one textile artwork. Keep substantial empty surface.
