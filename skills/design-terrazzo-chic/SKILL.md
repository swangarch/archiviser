# Design Terrazzo Chic

Build a complete interior language around terrazzo without allowing the aggregate to become the design. Keep the result modern, subtle, warm, elegant, functional, and materially specific.

## Core workflow

1. Inspect the source and lock camera, walls, openings, stairs, levels, ceiling, circulation, fixed joinery, and program unless change is authorized.
2. Classify the typology and decide where terrazzo performs as floor, stair, counter, wall panel, object, or a limited combination.
3. Set a quiet palette and aggregate scale.
4. Pair terrazzo with a controlled wood, glass, metal, textile, and plaster system.
5. Design furniture, lighting, joinery, service equipment, and small details around real use.
6. For multiple views, establish one approved inventory and translate it through each target geometry.
7. Validate circulation, material continuity, color balance, detail, and realism.

Read [references/prompt-framework.md](references/prompt-framework.md) when writing the production prompt.

## Keep terrazzo balanced

Use a warm ivory, cream, pale mineral, or similarly quiet base unless the project calls for another field. Keep aggregate controlled:

- small to medium chips at believable density;
- muted terracotta, dusty rose, sage, ochre, burgundy, charcoal, or similarly restrained accents;
- coherent scale across floors, stairs, counters, and objects;
- correct perspective, panel joints, thresholds, nosings, and expansion joints;
- low to moderate sheen with controlled highlights.

A useful default balance is 70–80% quiet warm neutrals, 15–25% wood and metal, and no more than 5–10% muted chromatic accents. Adjust to context rather than applying the ratio mechanically.

Avoid rainbow fields, oversized chips everywhere, repeated procedural speckles, loud Memphis styling, plastic shine, and using different terrazzo recipes in adjacent views of one project.

## Use ondulé metal with discipline

Interpret ondulé as finely corrugated, vertically ribbed, ripple-folded, or gently waved crafted metal. Use it for selected bar fronts, screens, balustrade panels, pedestals, cabinet fronts, or lighting components.

- Keep ribs fine and fabrication credible.
- Use satin stainless steel, champagne metal, warm bronze, or another restrained finish.
- Preserve crisp edges, fixings, returns, top rails, and junctions.
- Pair metal with terrazzo and wood so it reads as a precise accent.

Avoid mirror chrome, distorted circulation, sculptural waves on every surface, metallic clutter, and black-and-gold luxury clichés.

## Design furniture and detail around use

Do not leave a finished shell empty. Add a scaled, coherent furniture family and enough detail to make the program believable:

- compact chairs, tailored lounge pieces, slim stools, small tables, benches, and integrated seating;
- terrazzo, smoked glass, timber, leather, textile, and ribbed-metal combinations;
- restrained vases, trays, cups, glasses, bowls, books, coasters, napkins, and planting;
- human activity that confirms clearances, orientation, and social use.

Preserve negative space and circulation. Furniture must never block stairs, landings, doors, thresholds, or accessible turning areas.

## Build a credible café or bar

When the brief calls for a bar, design the service operation rather than adding bottles to a wall:

- locate the counter on a real landing or floor zone with service depth and customer clearance;
- use a durable terrazzo worktop and a restrained metal, timber, or mineral front;
- provide coherent backbar storage, glass shelves, task light, undercounter equipment, sink or espresso zone when plausible;
- add bottles, glassware, ceramics, tools, trays, menus without readable text, and staff activity at controlled density;
- coordinate stool count and table positions with circulation and egress.

Keep a daytime cultural café bright and welcoming when that suits the architecture. Do not default to dark nightlife, neon, oversized bottle displays, or hotel-lobby theatricality.

## Maintain one project across views

Use one inventory for terrazzo base and chips, wood tone, metal profile and finish, glass tint, upholstery colors, lighting family, stool model, table model, joinery, and service details. Let the target view control geometry and placement; never copy screen positions between cameras.

Compose with `match-archviz-multiview` when several images depict the same project and with `lock-architecture-shell` when construction must remain exact.

## Validate before delivery

Check that:

- architecture and camera remain fixed;
- terrazzo aggregate, joints, and scale are believable;
- the palette is colorful enough to feel intentional but not loud;
- ondulé metal is refined and constructionally credible;
- furniture creates a complete program without clutter;
- bar equipment and seating fit real circulation;
- materials, furniture, lighting, and details remain consistent across views;
- daylight stays warm-neutral rather than yellow;
- no shopping-mall, nightclub, generic café-chain, or luxury-hotel cliché appears.
