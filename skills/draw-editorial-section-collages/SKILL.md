# Draw Editorial Section Collages

Create an architectural drawing first and a collage second. Preserve exact spatial evidence while using cut paper, photography, print grain, halftone, and controlled color to make program, material, and inhabitation vivid.

## Core workflow

1. Inspect every input and identify the exact geometry source, edit target, material references, and entourage references.
2. Lock crop, projection, cut plane, walls, slabs, openings, levels, stairs, circulation, fixed joinery, furniture layout, planting zones, and program.
3. Reconstruct a complete, precise line drawing before adding collage fragments.
4. Establish flat cut poche and visible-edge hierarchy.
5. Assign a small material and print palette to named architectural planes.
6. Clip every paper or photographic fragment to its surface; add people and planting as separate cut layers.
7. Use overlap, scale, white edges, and print registration to create depth without simulated 3D lighting.
8. Validate geometry at thumbnail and full size before delivery.

Read [references/prompt-template.md](references/prompt-template.md) when preparing a production brief.

## Lock architectural evidence

Keep all shared edges, floor levels, voids, openings, stairs, rails, roof profiles, structural members, and furniture positions registered to the source. Use the cut plane consistently; reserve the strongest flat poche for construction actually intersected by it.

Do not use collage to hide unresolved geometry, bend straight edges, move openings, change floor heights, or invent an incoherent section.

## Build collage from discrete layers

- Linework: crisp black or charcoal CAD-like edges with controlled hierarchy.
- Cut construction: flat restrained poche without gradients or realistic shadow.
- Architectural materials: clipped fragments of concrete, cork, plywood, brick, stone, glass, fabric, or another specified finish.
- Planting: botanical photo cut-outs, silhouettes, photocopy fragments, or halftone layers with visible but refined edges.
- People and objects: monochrome or muted-color cut-outs, flat silhouettes, and occasional fine paper halos.
- Print surface: small controlled areas of scanned paper fiber, screenprint dots, photocopy grain, or slight ink misregistration.

Each material fragment must stop exactly at its architectural plane. Use a few fragments at intentionally varied print scales rather than one seamless texture covering the project.

## Keep the collage architectural

Use a restrained palette of warm white, charcoal, mineral gray, natural material tones, desaturated vegetation, and at most one clear accent color. Let structure, circulation, program, and the principal void determine the composition.

Avoid torn decorative borders, random rectangles, pasted lifestyle imagery, excessive botanical density, scrapbook nostalgia, mood-board composition, bright color, and texture on every surface.

## Avoid slipping back into rendering

Depth must come from line weight, overlap, clipped edges, paper contrast, halftone density, and limited flat tone. Remove seamless realistic materials, global illumination, ambient occlusion, soft cast shadows, bloom, reflections, atmospheric haze, watercolor wash, and continuous photographic lighting.

If the result still reads as a render with paper grain, return to the exact geometry source and rebuild only the collage layers.

## Validate before delivery

Check that:

- projection, cut plane, geometry, levels, openings, stairs, furniture, and program match the source;
- cut construction is stronger than visible architecture and collage fragments;
- material fragments are discrete, scaled deliberately, and clipped to correct planes;
- people and planting read as intentional cut layers rather than pasted mistakes;
- white space and the main spatial void remain clear;
- the palette stays low-saturation and accents remain controlled;
- depth comes from graphic layering rather than rendered light;
- the image reads as a precise architectural collage, not a photorealistic section, scrapbook, or mood board;
- no fake labels, dimensions, text, logos, or watermark appear.
