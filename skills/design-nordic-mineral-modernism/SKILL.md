# Design Nordic Mineral Modernism

Build calm northern architecture from mass, opening, light, and tactile contrast rather than a generic Scandinavian furniture palette. Let mineral construction ground the project while timber, metal, textiles, and inhabitation keep it precise and welcoming.

## Core workflow

1. Inspect the source and lock camera, walls, openings, levels, roof, circulation, fixed joinery, and program unless change is authorized.
2. Classify the climate, landscape, typology, scale, daylight direction, and required degree of shelter or openness.
3. Establish a coherent mineral hierarchy before selecting timber, metal, furniture, or decor.
4. Shape deep openings, thresholds, benches, stairs, and service elements as parts of the architecture.
5. Coordinate furniture, lighting, acoustics, and human activity with the room's structural and material rhythm.
6. Generate or edit one view at a time.
7. Validate spatial clarity, material scale, surface cleanliness, lighting balance, circulation, and photographic realism.

Read [references/prompt-framework.md](references/prompt-framework.md) when preparing a production prompt.

## Start with northern mass and landscape

Use granite, slate, pale brick, concrete, or another locally credible mineral material to define weight, shelter, and permanence. Relate walls, piers, floors, stairs, and window reveals to the building's structural grid and landscape exposure.

- Use deep openings and continuous benches to make wall thickness legible.
- Keep major floor and wall planes continuous enough for the room to read at a glance.
- Frame sea, harbor, island, forest, snowfield, or low sky as an architectural relationship rather than scenery pasted behind glazing.
- Preserve generous clear circulation and negative space in public rooms.

Avoid arbitrary stone feature walls, thin stone wallpaper, fragmented alcoves, decorative boulders, and unrelated material changes.

## Build a controlled mineral palette

Choose one principal mineral family and at most two supporting mineral finishes.

- Keep granite grain, brick modules, board marks, joints, thresholds, and slab sizes believable.
- Use honed, split, bush-hammered, sawn, or lightly sandblasted finishes where exposure and touch justify them.
- Keep surfaces clean and dry unless weathering is explicitly requested.
- Distinguish mass from touch surfaces: a rough outer pier may meet a honed sill, bench, rail, or counter.
- Use cool ash, pale oak, smoked timber, brushed stainless steel, dark steel, smoked glass, wool, linen, and leather as restrained counterpoints.

Avoid muddy gray grading, stained stone, procedural speckle, excessive roughness, glossy luxury stone, all-over timber, and treating every surface as the same material.

## Use light as a material

Begin with the real northern condition: low sun, cool overcast sky, long twilight, snow bounce, or maritime reflection.

- Keep daylight neutral and directional enough to reveal reveals, texture, and wall depth.
- Use warm-neutral pendants, task lights, and wall grazers locally rather than applying a yellow cast to the entire image.
- Preserve clean whites, readable shadow detail, and controlled highlights on stone, glass, and metal.
- Align pendants, tracks, and wall washers with tables, bays, and material surfaces.

Avoid gloomy underexposure, theatrical darkness, blue color casts, orange lamps everywhere, or calling a room Nordic by making it desaturated.

## Furnish public and private rooms deliberately

Use a small coherent furniture family with exact scale and robust silhouettes. Favor slate, oatmeal, charcoal, lichen, tobacco, and dark brown in low-saturation wool, leather, and woven textiles. Add ceramics, books, tableware, or planting only where they confirm use.

Do not default to a matching set of pale-wood chairs, generic hygge decor, tiny tables scattered across the floor, or an empty mineral shell. Keep entrances, windows, stairs, service zones, and accessible routes clear.

## Preserve contemporary northern character

Avoid cabins, saunas, hygge styling, generic Scandinavian living rooms, luxury ski resorts, brutalist gloom, and historic Nordic pastiche. The result may feel quiet and elemental, but it must remain spatially clear, materially specific, functional, clean, and inhabited.

## Validate before delivery

Check that:

- camera and fixed architecture respect the source;
- the room reads as one coherent spatial system;
- mineral materials have credible module, finish, jointing, and thickness;
- surfaces look intentional and clean rather than dirty or procedurally noisy;
- timber and metal clarify rather than overwhelm mineral mass;
- openings, thresholds, furniture, lighting, and circulation support the program;
- landscape and climate affect the architecture and daylight;
- white balance stays neutral, with warmth limited to local light and touch surfaces;
- the image avoids generic Scandinavian, hotel, cabin, and empty-minimalism clichés.
