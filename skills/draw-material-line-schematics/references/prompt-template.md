# Material-line schematic prompt

Use case: style-transfer or precise-object-edit.

Input image: exact edit target and binding geometry, projection, object-placement, and context source.

Primary request: Convert the source into a clean architectural schematic with a strict line hierarchy and extremely pale material notation.

Geometry lock: preserve crop, projection or camera, walls, slabs, openings, levels, stairs, railings, structure, fixed joinery, furniture, people, planting, and program.

Line hierarchy:

- strongest restrained black at actual cut construction;
- medium-fine visible architecture;
- lighter furniture, people, and planting;
- finest entourage detail;
- palest material notation, about 10–15 percent black in appearance.

Material inventory: name the floor, wall, ceiling, roof, glazing, and fixed-joinery finishes. Assign sparse joints, modules, courses, boards, panels, seams, stipple, or convention marks appropriate to each. Keep every pattern aligned, correctly scaled, and clipped to its surface.

Color: white background and black or gray linework. Add no color unless the user requests one or two flat diagram accents.

Avoid: material lines stronger than entourage, uniform line weight, dense hatch, checkerboard floors, gray fills, gradients, shadows, rendered light, reflections, photographic texture, paper grain, text, dimensions, labels, logos, and watermark.

Validation: compare geometry, line hierarchy, surface direction, joint scale, boundary clipping, floor continuity, ceiling restraint, and material legibility at both thumbnail and full size.
