# Board system

## Grid

Use a consistent modular grid across the set, commonly 8, 10, or 12 columns. Establish outer margins before columns, then keep gutters and baselines stable. Break the grid only to create a deliberate dominant image or drawing.

## Hierarchy

- Level 1: board number, project title, or dominant claim.
- Level 2: section heading and primary drawing title.
- Level 3: captions, legends, scale, and short technical notes.

Choose one dominant asset per board, two or three secondary groups, and quiet supporting evidence. Preserve adequate size for plans, sections, construction drawings, and text at print scale.

## Continuity

Keep background, type family, sizes, rules, numbering, drawing titles, caption positions, palette, and image grading consistent. Let plans align across boards when comparison matters. Do not repeat content merely to fill space.

## Concept text

Prefer a short evidence-based statement followed by three to five named strategies. Pair each strategy with the drawing or diagram that proves it. Avoid slogans unsupported by architecture.
