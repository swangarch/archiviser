# Modern timber prompt framework

Use case: style-transfer, sketch-to-render, or precise-object-edit.

Primary request: state whether the task is a full timber redesign, a structural refinement, a joinery correction, or a single finish change.

Geometry authority: lock camera, crop, walls, openings, levels, stairs, ceiling, circulation, fixed joinery, and approved objects.

Typology and scale: name the program, span, height, occupancy, daylight, acoustic needs, and likely structural system.

Structural hierarchy:

- primary posts, beams, frames, panels, arches, or trusses;
- secondary joists, ribs, purlins, blocking, and enclosure frames;
- integrated shelving, stairs, guards, furniture, and services.

Assembly language: specify two to four repeatable joint types, connector material, base and head conditions, tolerance gaps, and glass or mineral interfaces.

Material palette: structural timber species and tone; floor or touch-surface timber; plaster, stone, concrete, glass, metal, textiles; low-sheen finish.

Floor: state board size, laying axis, tone relationship, finish, thresholds, and patterns to avoid.

Lighting: neutral daylight, soft bounce, legible junctions, no yellow filter or glossy bloom.

Avoid: rustic cabin, temple imitation, decorative brackets, all-over cladding, orange varnish, repeated grain, impossible spans, random connectors, parametric clutter, architecture drift, text, logos, watermark.

Validation: compare geometry, load path, structural hierarchy, joint repetition, material interfaces, grain direction, floor grid, services, circulation, and realism.
