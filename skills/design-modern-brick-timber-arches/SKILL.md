# Design Modern Brick Arches + Timber

Build contemporary civic space from a legible masonry order softened by timber structure, lining, furniture, and touch surfaces. Treat every arch as construction with load, thickness, bearing, and rhythm rather than as a pasted historical motif.

## Core workflow

1. Inspect the source and lock camera, walls, openings, levels, circulation, structure, fixed joinery, and program unless change is authorized.
2. Establish the primary spatial axis, bay rhythm, arch type, pier width, springing line, structural depth, and load path.
3. Resolve the complete brick system before adding timber: wall thickness, arch rings, radial joints, reveals, bearings, mortar, edges, and openings.
4. Assign timber a specific role: secondary roof structure, ceiling lining, acoustic layer, shelving, benches, tables, doors, or hand-contact elements.
5. Coordinate daylight, concealed lighting, furniture, and occupation with the masonry bays.
6. Generate or edit one view at a time.
7. Validate symmetry where intended, construction credibility, material scale, spatial continuity, circulation, and neutral color balance.

Read [references/prompt-framework.md](references/prompt-framework.md) when preparing a production brief.

## Make the arch structurally legible

- Keep the apex, springing points, pier widths, and arch thickness geometrically consistent within one repeated order.
- Use semicircular, segmental, elliptical, catenary, or shallow relieving arches only when their span and support logic suit the project.
- Turn brick bond and radial joints through the soffit; show sufficient bearing at each springing point.
- Let deep reveals, wall thickness, and shadows explain mass without making the room gloomy.
- Use a concrete or steel ring beam only when the hybrid logic is visible and credible.

Do not use thin brick outlines, unsupported veneer arches, inconsistent curves, arbitrary half-arches, or asymmetry that reads as modeling failure. If the composition is axial, center the camera and verify both sides explicitly.

## Give brick and timber different jobs

Brick should define mass, enclosure, piers, arches, thermal depth, and long spatial rhythm. Timber should reduce scale and support occupation.

- Use credible beams, joists, slats, acoustic rafts, shelves, doors, tables, benches, or window linings.
- Size timber from span and load rather than visual drama; avoid huge decorative members.
- Show bearings, plates, gaps, brackets, or shadow joints where timber meets masonry.
- Keep species and finish coherent. Prefer matte oak, ash, chestnut, or another contextually plausible timber.
- Align ceiling slats, lights, shelves, and furniture with the architectural bay.

Avoid covering every surface in timber or using it as rustic decoration.

## Keep the space contemporary

Use precise reveals, large clear openings, restrained glazing, integrated services, simple furniture, and continuous circulation. Historical masonry knowledge may inform construction, but the result must not become a church nave, medieval cloister, Romanesque revival, nostalgic station, warehouse conversion, or themed restaurant.

In public rooms, provide enough books, tables, benches, handrails, lighting, and people to prove use without filling the hall. Preserve one readable spatial system rather than splitting the architecture into disconnected arch-shaped pods.

## Control brick color and light

- Use nuanced low-saturation red, brown-red, burgundy, or muted terracotta brick with coherent variation.
- Keep mortar depth and color believable; do not outline every brick too strongly.
- Use neutral daylight and local warm-neutral light. Keep whites and pale floors clean.
- Allow light to wash soffits, reveals, timber, and wall thickness without an orange filter.

Avoid dirty brick, procedural repetition, excessive weathering, dramatic darkness, orange grading, glossy sealers, and black-steel industrial clichés.

## Validate before delivery

Check that:

- the arch geometry is intentional and symmetrical wherever the order calls for symmetry;
- repeated bays share springing height, curve family, thickness, pier width, and alignment;
- masonry has credible bond, radial joints, bearing, depth, and load path;
- timber spans, member sizes, connections, and orientation are plausible;
- brick establishes mass while timber supports acoustics, occupation, and human scale;
- circulation, furniture, openings, and program remain clear;
- lighting reveals material depth without making the image yellow, dark, or nostalgic;
- the result reads as contemporary architecture rather than historical pastiche.
